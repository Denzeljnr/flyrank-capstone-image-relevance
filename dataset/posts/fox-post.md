# Why Red Foxes Thrive in Winter

When most animals are hunkering down to survive the cold, the red fox is
doing just fine. Of all the wild canines found across the Northern
Hemisphere, few are as well-adapted to snow and freezing temperatures as
this small, quick-footed predator.

## A coat built for the cold

A red fox's winter coat is thick, layered, and surprisingly effective at
trapping body heat. Even when the temperature drops well below freezing, a
fox can curl up in the open snow, wrap its bushy tail around its face and
paws, and stay warm enough to sleep comfortably outdoors. That tail isn't
just for balance — it's essentially a built-in blanket.

## Hunting by sound alone

One of the fox's most striking winter skills is its ability to hunt prey it
cannot see. Foxes can hear small rodents moving under a foot or more of
snow, and famously leap high into the air before diving nose-first into the
snowpack to catch them — a hunting technique researchers call "mousing."

## Solitary, but not lonely

Unlike wolves, red foxes are largely solitary hunters. They don't rely on a
pack to bring down prey, since their diet consists mostly of small mammals,
birds, and whatever fruit or scraps they can scavenge. This independence
lets them survive in a much wider range of habitats — from deep forest to
farmland to the edges of cities.

Red foxes are a reminder that thriving in harsh conditions doesn't always
require size or strength. Sometimes it just takes the right coat, sharp
ears, and a willingness to hunt alone.
