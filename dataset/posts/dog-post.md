# What 20,000 Years of Domestication Did to the Dog

Dogs are, genetically, still wolves — but tens of thousands of years living
alongside humans have reshaped nearly everything about how they look,
think, and behave.

## From scavenger to companion

The leading theory is that dogs didn't start as wolves humans tamed on
purpose. Instead, wolves that were less fearful of people likely began
scavenging near early human camps, and over generations, natural selection
favored the friendliest, most tolerant individuals. The relationship grew
from there — first practical, eventually deeply personal.

## Reading human faces

One of the clearest signs of how much dogs have changed is their ability to
read human expressions and gestures. Dogs will follow a pointed finger to
find food or a hidden object almost instinctively — something even wolves
raised by humans from birth struggle to do. It's a skill that seems to have
been selected for directly, because it made dogs more useful and easier to
live with.

## An enormous range of one species

From a tiny chihuahua to a massive mastiff, the physical variety within
domestic dogs is unmatched by almost any other mammal species. That
diversity is a direct result of selective breeding for specific jobs —
herding, guarding, hunting, or simply companionship — layered on top of the
same underlying wolf ancestry.

Dogs are proof of just how much intentional and unintentional selection can
reshape a species — turning a wild predator into the animal most likely to
be found asleep on the couch.
